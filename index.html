CHILE

"Land of Poets"

Chile, officially the Republic of Chile, is a long, narrow country in South America squeezed between the Andes Mountains and the Pacific Ocean. Known for its incredible natural beauty, hyper-arid deserts, spectacular ice fields, rich culture, and warm hospitality, Chile offers an incredible journey across unique ecosystems and centuries of history.

7 Natural Landscapes of Chile

Explore the beauty of Chile’s natural landscapes and breathtaking views using the arrows to slide through all 7 destinations.

1. Valle de la Luna

Type: Atacama Desert

Geographical Description: The Valle de la Luna located near San Pedro de Atacama in the Antofagasta Region of northern Chile, is a dry, rocky landscape with salt formations, sand dunes, cliffs, and ridges shaped by wind and water. Its unusual formations and appearance make it resemble the surface of the Moon.

2. Torres del Paine National Park

Type: National Park

Geographical Description: The Torres del Paine National Park located at the Chilean Patagonia, is a world-famous protected nature reserve. It is a southern Patagonian sanctuary marked by soaring granite horn peaks, massive blue glaciers, and sweeping steppes. The region carries bright blue lakes like Lago Grey and Lago Pehoé, fed directly by the massive Grey Glacier part of the Southern Patagonian Ice Field.

3. General Carrera Lakes

Type: Glacial lake

Geographical Description: The General Carrera Lakes located near the Chile–Argentina border. It is famous for its turquoise glacial waters and the Marble Caves, which were formed by water erosion over thousands of years. At about 1,850 km², it is the largest lake in Chile.

4. Easter Island (Rapa Nui)

Type: Remote Volcanic Island

Geographical Description: Easter Island (Rapa Nui) is the most isolated inhabited island in the world. Built by the Rapa Nui people between 1250 - 1500 AD, there are 900+ moai statues carved from volcanic tuff. Nobody knows exactly how they moved the statues without wheels or pulleys. The island is also a National Park and the Rapa Nui people still live there, protecting their culture.

5. Chiloé Island

Type: Temperate rainforest and Coastal island

Geographical Description: Chiloé Island is located at the Los Lagos Region of southern Chile. It is known for its beautiful landscapes, traditional wooden houses, colorful culture, and historic wooden churches. The island is also famous for its unique traditions, folklore, seafood, and natural scenery.

6. Valle del Maipo - Maipo Valley

Type: River Valley

Geographical Description: The Maipo Valley is a major wine-producing region near Santiago, Chile. It lies between the Andes Mountains to the east and the Coastal Range to the west. It is also known as the "Bordeaux of South America". Its climate and fertile valley landscapes make it an important area for grape growing and wine production.

7. Volcán Villarrica & Lake District

Type: Island National Park

Geographical Description: The Villarrica and Lake District is located in the Araucanía Region of southern Chile. It is known for Lake Villarrica, clear waters, forests, and the snow-covered Villarrica Volcano. The area is a popular destination for enjoying its lakes, mountains, and volcanic landscapes.

3 Human-Made Landscapes of Chile

Explore some of Chile’s historical man-made landscapes, where you can find historical architecture and the country's rich culture.

1. La Moneda Palace

Geographical Location: Santiago, Chile

Historical & Architectural Description: The La Moneda Palace, located in Santiago, Chile, is the official workplace of the President of Chile. It was designed by architect Joaquin Toesca and was opened in 1805 as the Royal Mint of Santiago. In 1846, it became the seat of the Chilean government. It was originally a coin-making factory before turning into a government seat. That’s how it got its name “Moneda” which means coin in Spanish. Today, it is one of the country's most important historical buildings. Visitors can admire its beautiful architecture and watch the Changing of the Guard ceremony.

2. Valparaíso Historic Quarter

Geographical Location: Valparaíso, Chile

Historical & Architectural Description: The Historic Quarter of Valparaíso, located on central Chile’s Pacific coast, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is famous for its colorful houses, steep hills, street art, and old cable cars called funiculars. The city has been an important seaport for many years and is known for its rich culture and history.

3. Cristo Redentor de los Andes (Christ the Redeemer of the Andes)

Geographical Location: Andes Mountains, on the Chile–Argentina border

Historical & Architectural Description: This statue of Christ, called the "Cristo Redentor de los Andes," located in the Andes Mountains on the Chile and Argentina border, was built in 1904 as a symbol of peace and friendship between Chile and Argentina after both countries settled a border dispute. It reminds people of the importance of unity and peace.
